## What changed on day 2

232 agent-arm decisions differ between the two day-2 runs: 208 change what the viewer gets (a different goal, or a page instead of nothing, or the reverse); in 24 the agent does nothing either way and only the recorded reason differs. Both runs advance the virtual clock by a fixed 5 ms per decision and the unseen day is built from a fixed seed, so from the same day-1 memory they compare exactly. Day 1 itself uses measured compute times; its timestamps differ by milliseconds between fresh runs, which could in principle move these counts slightly.

Goals the floor left alone on day 1 were tracked, so a prediction that was too low can be corrected; once the corrected score clears the floor, the agent pursues that goal on day 2. A changed first visit also changes what later visits by the same viewer do (continue the new goal, or leave its page live).

## Limits

- Day 2 has no simulated outcomes: the pack's simulator hard-codes the replay date, so whether the changed decisions did better is not measured here. A third day would judge them.
- Viewers not triggered on day 1 have no recorded activity between 30 September and day 2, so many look absent and qualify for "return after absence" (G12). That is a gap in the data, not a choice of the agent.
- One day of holdout data moves the baseline only where the evidence is large (G07, n=361); small cells barely move, by design.
